## On the radar

Some news items that caught tech workers’ attention this month:

- “Everything is too expensive nowadays.” [Workers in the cleaning sector went on strike](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rbNOuQAZyS0) for better wages.
- [Port workers in the Rotterdam harbour went on strike on 4 September](https://www.joc.com/article/port-strikes-hit-europe-as-schedule-reliability-drops-to-single-digits-6281165) to pressure the Dutch government to withdraw a proposacuts to social security and pensions.
- Uber workers in the Netherlands, together with coworkers from Belgium, France, Germany, Poland, Romania and the UK, [file a class action suit against the company](https://www.taxi-point.co.uk/post/uber-faces-collective-action-over-dynamic-pay-as-uk-drivers-claim-5-300-annual-income-reduction). The workers claim compensation for missed earnings (on average €7,500 a year in lost income) and breaches of data protection laws due to Uber’s allegedly manipulative pay-setting algorithm.
- [Workers at Swedish contact centre company Transcom reject its proposal for mass layoff arrangements](https://uniglobalunion.org/news/transcoms-to-exit-netherlands-workers-jobs/) (‘social plan’). Transcom wants to fire around 150 workers and shut its site in the Netherlands after 26 years, to expand operations elsewhere.
- Airbus staff [strike for remote work options and win](https://www.theguardian.com/business/2026/aug/21/airbus-bows-remote-working-strikes).
- Incidentally (not incidentally), it turns out that fully remote workers report the highest levels of well-being, and are less likely to quit, compared to partially remote or fully on-site workers, [a survey among 7k+ healthcare workers found](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1848857).
- Anna’s Archive calls on people to scan and upload books, [to prevent AI companies from destroying physical copies](https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/artificial-intelligence/worlds-largest-open-library-calls-for-volunteers-to-scan-and-preserve-physical-books-as-ai-companies-buy-scan-and-destroy-them-annas-archive-says-time-is-running-out-as-knowledge-is-permanently-monopolized-on-private-servers) in the process of feeding the hungry LLM monsters.
- Google search is dying. [The AI summaries replacing it are worse.](https://thewalrus.ca/google-search-is-dying/)
- A short answer from a human is worth more than a wall of text from a robot, even if the robot is right. [Don’t paste the AI.](https://dontpastetheai.com/) Really. [Stop pasting the AI at me.](https://dontpastetheai.com/angry/)
- From security workers to the world: “You cannot do age or identity verification safely. It always creates some sort of record and that set of records will always become a target.” This after [supposedly ‘secure’ processor IDScan turns out to have been leaking a live feed of a total of 153 million identity documents to hackers for over a year](https://www.techdirt.com/2026/09/03/hackers-had-a-live-feed-of-every-id-this-verification-company-scanned-for-over-a-year/).
- Twitter uh sorry [X issues a cease-and-desist notice to open source tools Nitter and XCancel](https://www.tweaktown.com/news/113272/xcancel-and-nitter-receive-cease-and-desist-letters-from-x-over-alleged-scraping/index.html) that allow you to bypass the company’s registration wall. End of an era? [Not so fast](https://github.com/zedeus/nitter/commit/1428b4c2b4246f92a7e5b2673438e5fb39fcc4a3).
- Norway’s Forbrukerrådet (Consumer Council) describes [a day in the life of an ensh\*ttificator](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=T4Upf_B9RLQ) (ensh\*ttifier?).
- [LOW←TECH MAGAZINE](https://solar.lowtechmagazine.com/power/) is a solar powered website. Bad weather? Then the site’s offline.
